# Weizenbaum’s Creation as a Pandora's Box: How ELIZA has paved a way for modern AI Chatbots

[[Eliza]] is a foundational piece of technology whose existence echoes through the modern development of artificial intelligence. Joseph [[Weizenbaum]]’s simple chatbot ELIZA that was created in 1966 to mimic a therapist still resonates through most modern AI development creating a lasting impact that has continued through generations. ELIZA revealed the terrifying ease of how humans quickly become comfortable with interacting with computers in more personal and intimate ways. As we enter a new frontier of [[ChatGPT]] and AI induced psychosis it is evident that there is a necessity to understand where these chatbots originated from and their original roles compared to what they have been developed into. Looking back on ELIZA’s ability to gain the trust of humans early on in her development one can see how this digital/human relationship has been weaponized by tech companies to prey on people and their gullibility as a way to gather and then sell their data. 
<p>In the book <i>Inventing ELIZA<i> by Sarah Ciston, et al. the history of ELIZA is broken down and the smoke and mirrors are cleared from ELIZA’s creation and how she works. Quickly, Ciston, et al. explain some of the dangers of AI explaining: 
<p>“Computers have no sense of morality, no sense of justice or rights, and they certainly are unable to take on a responsibility toward others. Though they might appear to be intelligent, they are not. Though they might appear to be sympathetic, they are not. Instead, they are instrumental, technical, and calculating, not just about mathematics or computing but about everything, including human life, feelings, and communities.” (Ciston et al. 18) 
<p>Though this may seem self-explanatory, this is central when beginning to understand how the roles that AI plays in modern society and why their warm welcome into everyday, mundane parts of life is worrisome. The goals of these artificial beings are to simply mimic, comfort, and keep the computer-user talking and coming back. ELIZA was not one singular system or personality but a series of different versions that were interchangeably used for different situations when needed. This system has been further developed and used in modern chatbots, it is the foundational programming that is used to trick the user into feeling connected to them or as if they were a real conscious being that is being conversed with. In the book, <i>The AI Matrix: Profits, Power, Politics<i> by Daniel Mügge, et al, they explain how data is the new frontier, something that is more profitable than ever because of the modern ability to gather analytics from chatbot usage and other forms of AI. Data is explained as:
<p>
 “Scholars likened it to a raw material (like oil extracted from fossil reservoirs) or something cultivated (like crops on a farm). Yet others portrayed data as analogous to land: a territory to be explored, mapped and enclosed like private property to generate rents. Irrespective of which metaphor fits best, they jointly highlight how data access has been crucial for AI’s technical and economic breakthrough.” (Mügge, et al. 10) 

<p>In the past, as AI was first being developed it was to see if machines could really mimic humans and problem solve, now it is evolved. Modern development of AI has answered the question of if it can mimic or problem solve and now it is being used as a tool by tech companies to see how much data and information can be gathered willingly from humans. Recently, AI has been packaged as something that humans <i>need<i> rather than something that can be used; it is pushed onto everyone: students, businesses, the arts, it does not matter. The lines continue to be blurred as this false idea of necessity is constantly being sold to people. Weizenbaum’s simple chatbot ELIZA has paved the way for future generations of modern chatbots and the outcome is disheartening as more and more people fall for the illusions of AI without seeing what it is taking in return.
