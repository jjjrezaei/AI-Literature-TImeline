# Nausicää and the Valley of the Wind 

Hayao Miyazaki’s 1984 film Nausicaa of the Valley of the Wind is an important addition to the discussion of artificial intelligence through the attempted resurrection of a Giant Warrior. This animated film is set in a time when the world is nearly uninhabitable, due to toxins in the air that make it impossible to breathe. There are few spots where people manage to live, one of which is the valley. The way that the wind works in the valley makes it possible for people to breathe safely. The protagonist, Nausicaa, is a girl from this valley that cares deeply for the world and the people around her. During the film, a ship from another nation (unfriendly one at that), lands in the valley. On this ship is the embryo of a Giant Warrior (also called God Warriors), which were powerful beings that existed long ago. They are the reason that the world is a wasteland, since they had previously destroyed it. 

The leader of the other nation, Kushana, goes through great strides to try and resurrect the Giant Warrior, and toward the end of the movie, brings it out to fight the Ohm, who are wild creatures that live in the toxic wasteland. However, the Giant Warrior has not been nurtured enough, and cannot withstand fighting on its own. Kushana commands the Giant Warrior to destroy the Ohm, and the Giant Warrior fires a few shots before dissolving into itself. 

This Giant Warrior is relevant to our discussion of AI for two reasons. The first is that this film shows what happens when a being is not nurtured to completion and immediately put to use for laborious tasks. AI is used our advantages, but is not being taken care of or maintained. In this film, the Giant Warrior could not sustain the requests of Kushana and collapsed in on itself because of the demands that it was not ready for. This raises the question of strain on a system (AI) that may not be ready to meet constant and high load demands. 

The second reason that this film is relevant is because of the command-obedience dynamic that is seen between Kushana and the Giant Warrior. The Giant Warrior never questions what it is asked to do; it simply executes the task. Similar to chatbot AI models, this is a sequence of request and response. Is response enough to qualify a being as intelligent or is there something more to intelligence? As Antonio Damasio explores in Chapter 11 of Descartes’ Error, emotions and interaction with the environment are key parts of reasoning and logic. This Giant Warrior carries none of these traits, as shown by its lack of questioning and curiosity throughout the film. Despite having a body to interact with the environment, it does not appear to experience the world. Whether this being even has a brain is unknown. Similar to this Giant Warrior, artificial intelligence does not possess emotions, even if responses may seem like it does. Unlike the Giant Warrior, artificial intelligence does not have a way to experience the world through a physical body, which makes it a step further from Damasio’s proposal of comprehension and intelligence. 

Damasio, Antonio. “A Passion for Reasoning.” Descartes’ Error, Avon Books, New York, 1994, pp. 245–252. 
Miyazaki, Hayao, director. Nausicää and the Valley of the Wind. Studio Ghibli, 1984.
