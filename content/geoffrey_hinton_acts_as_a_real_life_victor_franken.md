# Geoffrey Hinton acts as a real-life Victor Frankenstein, abandoning his creation when it got too real

Mary Shelley's Frankenstein operates as a cautionary tale illustrating the dangers of scientific ambition, human greed, and lack of ethical oversight when creating new technologies released to the public. This peice of literature should be examined to understand AI and its relationship to humans because it reveals the perils of abandoning dangerous man-made creations, the drastic effects they have, and the idea that human creations will eventually turn on their creator. As seen in the text, “Unable to endure the aspect of the being I had created, I rushed out of the room, and continued a long time traversing my bed-chamber, unable to compose my mind to sleep (Shelley, pg 62).” This moment captures the moment Victor Frankenstein witnesses his creation coming to life and his reaction to his scientific discovery. After countless sleepless nights and hard-working days, devoting his life to this creature, the reality of what he did was too unbearable. His first reactions were fear, disgust, and horror, revealing the novel's main tragedy: Victor's unaccountability for his creation, which leads to the suffering of the creature, the creator, and innocent people who get involved. This moment showcases the dreadfulness Victor feels after facing the realities of what he has done and the being he has created. It was too much for him, so he stormed off and left, leaving his creation alone, running away from any responsibility. This leads to this new species being released into society without any oversight or supervision, similar to how Artificial Intelligence and ChatGPT have been unleashed to the public. In our own world of scientific creations and Artificial Intelligence, this connects to Geoffrey Hinton, named the “godfather of AI” due to his pioneering work and crucial role in the development of the AI that we all know today. Just like Victor, he abandoned the work he slaved over for decades, right when it became real and a threat, and felt dread after facing the realities of what he had done and the being he had created. As seen in The New York Times article, "The Godfather of AI’ Leaves Google and Warns of Danger Ahead," "Hinton said he has quit his job at Google, where he has worked for more than a decade and became one of the most respected voices in the field, so he can freely speak out about the risks of AI. A part of him, he said, now regrets his life’s work (Metz, pg 1).” The language of 'regret’ ties to how Victor felt after his work on making his creation. Both scientists worked so hard, not realizing the dangers of their discoveries, revealing the dangers of scientific ambition and creation. Geoffrey Hinton acts as a real-life Victor Frankenstein, abandoning his creation when it got too real. This ties into the longer history of Artificial Intelligence, scientific ambition, and abandonment, and can be investigated in conversation with Mary Shelley's novel Frankenstein.
