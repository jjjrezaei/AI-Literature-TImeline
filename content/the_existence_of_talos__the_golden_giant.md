# The Existence of Talos, the Golden Giant

Hephaestus, the Greek god of invention and blacksmithing, created a giant bronze man named Talos, commissioned by Zeus to protect the island of Crete from invaders. His duty was to patrol the island every day and, to defend the island, hurled boulders at approaching enemies. First mentioned "around 700 B.C. by Hesiod" (Shashkevich), Talos becomes one of the earliest conceptions of a humanoid robot. Hephaestus was most known for his creation of automata: giant, humanoid like robots constructed for admiration/gifts or protection. What allowed Talos to come to life and move about was a life-fluid known as ichor, the "blood of the gods" that ran through a singular tube or vein that connected from his head to his foot. Talos suffered the same vulnerability as Achilles in the sense that "he faced certain death if one merely punctured his ankle" (Talos, greekmyth.com) where the ichor ran through him. Hephaestus was widely known for manufacturing automata devices that could "act[ing] of one's own will" (Binkley & Loftus qtd. in Christodoulou & Tsoucalas), which in turn started the creations of the first artificially developed, intelligent robots.                                                                                                    1.
