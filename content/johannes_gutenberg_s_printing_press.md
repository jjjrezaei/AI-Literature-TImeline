# Johannes Gutenberg's Printing Press

In 1454, Gutenberg utilized his printing press for commercial use. The origins of printing, such as block printing, trace back to China. However, in 1454, Gutenberg created the first press that led to printing in large numbers. However, this was only possible due to the migration of paper from Marco Polo. 300 years pass as Gutenberg’s press revolutionizes the world of texts and literature. Using ink and movable type, Gutenberg utilized the concept of a press, which was once used to make food and wine in the Mediterranean. The initial purpose of increased publication by the Gutenberg Press was for a subsequent increase in widespread education and literacy on economics; it evolved into even more as time went on. Another main proponent behind major circulation came from the church; with a lack of funds, Gutenberg was pressured to print Bibles, something in which he is now renowned. Prior to Gutenberg’s press, books were written by hand. Meaning they were not only rare but also expensive. Furthermore, the use of metal letters, almost like a stamp, was able to be pressed into a page over and over again, which improved the rate of printing to around 250 one-sided pages per hour (Augustyn). 

Without Gutenberg’s precedent in printing, books such as Paradise Lost by John Milton (1667), Plutarch’s Lives by Plutarch (early 2nd century AD), The Sorrows of Werther by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe (1774), and Frankenstein by Mary Shelley (1818) couldn’t have been so accessible and circulated. The former three books can be used to describe the formation of artificial intelligence through Mary Shelley’s literary creature, which was created by Victor Frankenstein. In the novel, we witness The Creature gain the ability to express himself through reading books and observing a family in a cottage. These books couldn’t have existed in an inexpensive and maintainable format, especially taken from a poor family living isolated from the nearby village without Gutenberg’s press and the innovation it brought about. Gutenberg allowed for the circulation of ideas in a way that hadn’t existed before. This ultimately leads to a domino effect. Without the sharing of scientific ideals due to the Gutenberg press, computation couldn’t have been invented in the same amount of time. Printing also predates the digitization of texts and literature. Once printing started efficiently with Gutenberg and computers were later created, the digitization of these prints came next. With the digitization of texts, AI and LLMs are now able to scrape texts and mimic a human voice. Something that couldn’t exist without the source material. In short, the Gutenberg printing press led to the circulation of ideas, which in turn powers AI’s content creation. Not only do texts inform AI of language patterns, but also of common strings of phrases. Without the mass circulation of ideas and language, AI would have nothing to work off of. Language, especially written language, wouldn’t be accessible in the way that it is today without Gutenberg’s printing press. 

Gutenberg worked to solidify the book as an object, something which Amaranth Borsuk expands upon. She writes that the book is not only an object, but it is also content, an idea, and an interface. When we take these four aspects into account, we can see a direct correlation between the beginning of the book (reached with the help of Johannes Gutenberg), and the beginning of A.I. In Borsuk’s written history, we see countless factors that lead to this, but one honorable mention includes Gutenberg. Without his commercialization and infamous court case, many wouldn’t know the history of the press/fonts. Neither would they attribute his work as a precursor to the current language and books we have today. All in all, A.I., which scrapes language and literature from the web, wouldn’t be feasible without the initial creation of physical (and later digitized) books. These books were made possible by countless events and intellectuals, as well as notably by Johannes Gutenberg’s innovations in the printing press.
