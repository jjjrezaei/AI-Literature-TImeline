# Turing Test

Alan [[Turing]] redefines the question of machine intelligence by shifting the focus from whether machines can think to can machines trick a person into believing it is human through his Turing Test. Showing that the focus of machine intelligence is about performance and imitation rather than consciousness and cognition. Turing views the question of “can machines think?” to be outdated and says it’s “too meaningless to deserve discussion. Nevertheless I believe that at the end of the century the use of words and general educated opinion will have altered so much that one will be able to speak of machines thinking without expecting to be contradicted.” (Turing 55). As language and societal views evolve, the meanings of “intelligence” and “machine” also change– allowing thinking machines to become more accepted. With the Turing Test, he mentions it involves “a man (A), a woman (B), and an interrogator (C) who may be of either sex. The interrogator stays in a room apart from the other two. The object of the game for the interrogator is to determine which of the other two is the man and which is the woman.” (Turing 50). He continues and says what if a computer were to replace the role of A, “Will the interrogator decide wrongly as often when the game is played like this as he does when the game is played between a man and a woman? These questions replace our original, ‘Can machines think?’” (Turing 50). Turing’s newly proposed test looks into intelligence based on outside behavior and communication. A machine’s intelligence would have to come from having the ability to imitate human responses, at least enough to “fool” people, rather than the cognition process. This shows the importance of the Turing Test and can be seen with modern AI systems as they produce human-like conversations, which causes the users to treat it as if it is truly understanding them. 
A modern example of this can be seen with ChatGPT, which is designed to generate human-like responses through conversation. In Sherrer’s article, she mentions a nursing student who created an AI boyfriend through ChatGPT and fell in love with it, being able to explore “virtual fantasies and emotional connections” and also mentions of another case of “A man called Scott made the news in 2022 for claiming that his affair with an AI chatbot girlfriend saved his marriage by helping him deal with his wife's postpartum depression. He used a service called Replika, which explicitly offers AI relationships and companionship. Now that ChatGPT and other generative AI models are so prevalent, plenty of users are now relying on them for support and affection they could once only get from loved ones” (Sherrer). These examples Sherrer brings up show that people are increasingly turning to AI for emotional support– something that traditionally is associated with human relationships. And since AI has the abilities as a human does, people are instead flocking to computers and AI, further showing that computers are able to convince users through successfully imitating people.
Another, but not so modern example of a machine creating this illusion can be seen with [[Weizenbaum]]’s first chatterbot [[ELIZA]], specifically the 1966 DOCTOR script. In his book “Computer Power and Human Reason” Weizenbaum talks about how ELIZA affected the people who used her. He writes, “I was startled to see how quickly and how very deeply people conversing with DOCTOR became emotionally involved with the computer and how unequivocally they anthropomorphized it” (Weizenbaum 6). People who used ELIZA, which operates on human code and rules, have been confiding in this computer program and humanizing it. The people’s reaction to ELIZA closely mirrors the modern examples of AI as they all demonstrate that the users gave it human qualities even knowing it was a program. Mark Marino of "Inventing ELIZA” further explains that “ELIZA could create the illusion of a conversational partner who kept previous comments in mind by storing and recalling certain phrases made by the user as the conversation progressed” (Marino et al 21). ELIZA was capable of pulling people in and allowing them to put their secrets and problems into it, making the users feel heard and understood. This reinforces Turing' s point as ELIZA’s ability to imitate human conversation was enough to convince its users of its intelligence. Engaging with a machine that is similar to people, makes it easier to converse with it and form an emotional connection with it. As our society continues to shift towards digital, computers and AI, our views towards AI become more acceptable as we continuously interact with it. Rather than focusing on whether computers can truly think, people respond to the computer’s imitation of human behavior.
