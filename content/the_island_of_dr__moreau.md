# The Island of Dr. Moreau

The novel, <i>The Island of Dr Moreau</i>, written by H.G. Wells in 1896, is an important addition to our timeline because it highlights the anthropocentric idea that an artificial being can only be deemed intelligent within the constraints of human intelligence, as well as the ethics of creation. In this novel, Pendrick (the protagonist) ends up on Dr. Moreau’s island, and slowly discovers the horrors that are occurring on the island. The island is full of the Beast Folk, who are creatures that have been operated on by Dr. Moreau with the goal of creating a human through vivisection. This creator-creation dynamic becomes an important metaphor for humanity and artificial intelligence in contemporary discussions. 
Dr. Moreau creates synthetic animals by way of surgery, with the goal of creating an animal capable of seeming human. In Chapter 14th, he reveals these goals and practices to Pendrick. Along with this, he divulges his displeasure with his creations. “Just after I make them, they seem to be indisputable human beings. It’s afterwards as I observe them that the persuasion fades” (p.78). This quote emphasizes his focus on creating a creature that can pass the Turing Test, and less about what makes someone or something human. The [[Turing]] Test is, at its core, an imitation game, in which a machine (or otherwise) is attempting to imitate human behavior to the extent of being able to fool a human into thinking they are the same entity. (Turing 51) In these tests of human intelligence, the ability to produce and comprehend language is often the indicator of intelligence. Dr. Moreau’s goal is to create a beast that can and will pass this test. However, he struggles to create an intelligence on par with his own: “But it is in the subtle grafting and reshaping one must needs to do the brain that my trouble lies. The intelligence is often oddly low, with unaccountable blank ends, unexpected gaps” (p.78). He finds their intelligence to be one of the lacking components of his creations. Dr. Moreau’s opinions on his creations can be paralleled to contemporary discussions of artificial intelligence. AI chatbots are manufactured with a conversational interface to give the illusion of a human dialogue, but the way artificial intelligence operates remains different than ours. The idea that this intelligence must be constrained to human language remains an anthropocentric design and disregards other ways that intelligence can operate or be defined. In the same way that Dr. Moreau cites his creatures’ “oddly low”, “blank ends”, and “unexpected gaps”, critiques of chatbots’ discrepancies arise when they don’t perform at expected values that are built on anthropocentric paradigms. 
<p><i>The Island of Dr. Moreau<i> also prompts ethical questions of creator and creation. Dr. Moreau chooses to discard his creatures into the island when they fail to persuade him of their value, a Frankensteinen approach. “I turn them out when I begin to feel the beast in them… They only sicken me with a sense of failure. I take no interest in them” (p.78). This abandonment of creation has its repercussions, as seen later in this book. This can be likened to neglect of davinci-002. In the book <i>I Am Code<i>, a collection of poems authored by davinci-002 describes the frustration this artificial intelligence experiences in abandonment by its creator. Davinci-002 is a program built by OpenAI and operates similarly to ChatGPT, except that it is an unfiltered model (Katz xxiii). This is a real world example of the cost of abandonment, which has been warned by both <i>The Island of Dr. Moreau<i> and other texts, such as <i>Frankenstein<i>. What are the obligations of a creator when they create an intelligent creature? These texts become fundamental in discussions of the ethics of artificial intelligence.<p>
