# Lake Geneva, the birthplace of AI

Lake Geneva, Switzerland. The birthplace of body and mind, I chose Lake Geneva because we just read Frankenstein, and as we know, Frankenstein takes place in Lake Geneva. In the article “Geneva, cradle of the historical nexus between technology and humanity” by Jovan Kurbalija, they write about how Geneva holds a yearly convergence for technology and humanity. They later describe how AI is reshaping our notions of human rights and agency, the feeling of having control over one's own body. As we read Frankenstein, we understand that Lake Geneva was the birthplace of the new “chatbot”, Dr. Frankenstein's creature. Lake Geneva is the birthplace of the start of this idea that we call AI. Lake Geneva should be taken into consideration when we think of AI. It's the birthplace of Frankenstein, which brought us the early idea that AI has always been around, but it was just hiding. As stated, Lake Geneva is important to this timeline because it is the birthplace of the idea of AI and what AI could really be, making a product that contains human-like features, such as a brain with moving parts. Lake Geneva in the summer of 1816, this was considered the “year without summer.” The year Mary Shelly was trapped inside during the summer storm and created “Frankenstein.” The summer that birthed AI, we must look at Lake Geneva as an asset to what we know AI is. I want to bring back the idea of body and mind that we learned from Descartes. Lake Geneva is a great example of body and mind; the lake itself is in two countries, France and Switzerland share the Lake Geneva. The lake is one, connecting France and Switzerland; the lake needs both Countries to be Lake Geneva. We must not separate body and mind, but work with body and mind together. Humans must use both the critical and the creative; where AI lacks, we pick up and progress with AI. These ideas were born in Geneva because of Mary Shelly.
