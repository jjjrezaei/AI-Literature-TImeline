# <i>Metal Gear Solid 2: Sons of Liberty</i>

The well known <i>Metal Gear Solid</i> series tackles many political and philosophical topics that become more and more relevant as time progresses. Following the exploration of genetics, nuclear disarmament, and deception in one of the most iconic video games of all time, Hideo Kojima’s <i>Metal Gear Solid</i> (1998), the 2001 sequel <i>Metal Gear Solid 2: Sons of Liberty</i> expands on these issues in their application to an increasingly digital age. This game, a type of interactive fiction, from twenty-five years ago has proven to be extremely prescient of modern virtual reality, simulation, and artificial intelligence, especially in their role in military training and information control. The game also displays elements of the [[Turing]] Test through its imitation of events from the first game (and some fourth-wall breaks) to trick both in-game characters and players into doing what the AI, and the game, wants us to do. As a result, freedom of thought and action, the discovery of truth, and even self identity, are put at risk when AI is implemented to filter information for us.
<p><h2>VR Training & Simulation </p></h2> <p>The <i>MGS</i> games have serious plots, mainly dealing with terrorists and nukes, but they can become very complicated with their many moving parts, usually initiated by characters that are constantly deceiving each other and/or just being downright goofy (e.g: a cowboy, a vampire, and an otaku Oppenheimer). But for the purposes of this discussion on artificial intelligence, the only external information you need to know is that Solid Snake, the character you play as in the first game, is revered as a legendary soldier.</p> <p><i>MGS2</i> takes place in 2007 and 2009. After the events in the introductory 2007 chapter, Solid Snake has to go into hiding after wrongfully being labeled a terrorist. In the 2009 chapter that takes up the majority of the game, you play as the VR trained soldier Raiden as you are sent into an oil rig-type facility to save the President from a group of terrorists known as Dead Cell. Raiden takes a lot of pride in his VR training as he says “I’ve completed three hundred missions in VR. I feel like some kind of legendary mercenary.” But in a later conversation with Snake (who is at the same facility undercover as Pliskin), we get insight into the problems with this kind of training:</p> <p><b>Raiden</b>: I’ve had extensive training – the kind that’s indistinguishable from the real thing.</p> <p><b>Pliskin</b>: Like what?</p> <p><b>Raiden</b>: Sneaking mission 60, Weapons 80,</p> <p><b>Pliskin</b>: VR, huh.</p> <p><b>Raiden</b>: But realistic in every way.</p> <p><b>Pliskin</b>: A virtual grunt of the digital age. That’s just great.</p> <p><b>Raiden</b>: That’s far more effective than live exercises.</p> <p><b>Pliskin</b>: You don’t get injured in VR, do you? Every year, a few soldiers die in field exercises.</p> <p><b>Raiden</b>: There’s a pain sensation in VR, and even a sense of reality and urgency. The only difference is that it isn’t actually happening.</p> <p><b>Pliskin</b>: That’s the way they want you to think, to remove you from the fear that goes with battle situations. War as a video game – what better way to raise the ultimate soldier?</p> <p><b>Raiden</b>: So you’re saying VR training is some kind of mind control?</p> <p>From this conversation, it’s clear that Raiden doesn’t grasp the true difference between reality and simulation. In Jean Baudrillard’s <i>Simulation and Simulacra</i>, he identifies the army as one of the “favored terrains of simulation” since “military psychology draws back from Cartesian certainties and hesitates to make the distinction between true and false… it is what today again outflanks them, submerging the principle of truth” (3). Raiden is a prime example of this phenomenon explained by Baudrillard. The desire for subservient soldiers who’ll blindly follow orders is as old as human history, but the element of simulation and actual application of VR training makes it so that these soldiers won’t even be aware of the true nature of their actions. For Raiden, and many real people that grew up playing games like <i>Call of Duty</i> and <i>Battlefield</i>, the perception of “War as a video game”, and their accolades in those games, has influenced their beliefs that they can be some kind of legendary soldier.</p> <p><h2>Brief Important Context</h2></p> <p>Simulation doesn’t end at Raiden’s prior VR training; in fact, the entire 2009 chapter is a simulation of the first game up until the end. For fans who have played the first game, they’ll recognize the constant coincidences such as the aquatic infiltration of a terrorist base, the assistance from a mysterious cyborg ninja, and the capture and torture of Snake/Raiden. At first this may be written off as the game just following a formula, maybe even attributed to laziness of the developers. But in the final act of the game, it’s revealed that these similarities are intentional and vital to the plans of the antagonists. Before going into the explanations of the simulation, there’s some important characters and plot elements to know without getting too much into the conspiracy and details of it:</p> <p>-The United States democracy isn’t real and the country is actually run by a secret organization called “the Patriots”</p>
<p>-The facility you infiltrate is a cover for Arsenal Gear, a massive submarine-type fortress</p>
<p>-The leader of the Dead Cell is Solidus Snake, he wants to seize Arsenal Gear to overthrow the Patriots</p>
<p>-Arsenal Gear houses an AI called GW</p>
<p>-Raiden was taking orders from the AI, posing as the Colonel and his girlfriend Rose, this whole time</p>
<p>-Ocelot is a triple agent working for the Patriots but actually wants to overthrow them (I think? He’s very complicated)</p> <p><h2>The S3 Plan</h2></p> <p>In an encounter with Solidus and Ocelot, they both mention the S3 Plan which “stands for Solid Snake Simulation… It’s a development program to artificially reproduce Solid Snake, the perfect warrior,” further establishing the goals of using VR and simulation to make legendary soldiers. However, their repetitive explanations are further revealed to be false definitions of the S3 simulation. In one of the final conversations with the AI Colonel and Rose, just before the final boss fight with Solidus, we are shown the true (for real this time) nature of S3 in its goal to control the flow of information: “The S3 Plan does not stand for Solid Snake Simulation. What it does stand for is Selection for Societal Sanity… The S3 is a system for controlling human will and consciousness.” The way this AI operates is the same as modern algorithms that create harmful echo chambers, distorting truth:</p> <p><b>Colonel</b>: You exercise your right to “freedom” and this is the result. All rhetoric to avoid conflict and protect each other from hurt. The untested truths spun by different interests continue to churn and accumulate in the sandbox of political correctness and value systems.</p> <p><b>Rose</b>: Everyone withdraws into their own small gated community, afraid of a larger forum. They stay inside their little ponds, leaking whatever “truths” suits them into the growing cesspool of society at large.</p> <p><b>Colonel</b>: The different cardinal truths neither clash nor mesh. No one is invalidated, but nobody is right.</p> <p><b>Rose</b>: Not even natural selection can take place here. The world is being engulfed in “truth.”</p> <p>This is the exact situation we are living in thanks to our own custom feeds. The For You page validates and reinforces your opinions based on the data it has on you. The influence of the feed is common knowledge by now, but an important aspect to take note of here is the mention of natural selection. The AI especially evokes the evolutionary approach of <i>Dr. Moreau</i> as it claims “Just as in genetics, unnecessary information and memory must be filtered out to stimulate the evolution of the species.” While there is truth to the harms of trivial information overload, the danger in <i>MGS2</i> and our current world is that the S3, and our own algorithms, do most of the filtering for us. In granting these algorithms all the power to “create context,” certain narratives arise and guide our perceptions on truth and events in the real world. <i>MGS2</i> answers Alan Turing’s question “can a computer, communicating over a teleprinter, fool a person into believing it is human?” (49) with a resounding yes; as the game’s structure evolves from a plan to reproduce the perfect soldier, to a plan to guide evolution, Raiden and players alike are shown how easily these machines can trick us into believing them when we place such blind faith in them.</p> <p><h2>Full Conversation with AI</h2></p> <p><iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/eKl6WjfDqYA?si=BlqJn09gHT6avrEs" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p> <p><h2>Maintaining our Humanity</h2></p>After going through this entire game of complicated conspiracies to produce soldiers and control the populace, we arrive at a potential answer that Joseph [[Weizenbaum]] would likely be supportive of. In <i>Computer Power and Human Reason</i>, he argues against the adoption of purely logical thinking to solve human problems, insisting that “there are some acts of thought that <i>ought</i> to be attempted only by humans… Even murderous wars have come to be perceived as mere problems to be solved by hordes of professional problemsolvers” (Weizenbaum 13). <i>MGS2</i> similarly warns against this arithmetical approach to military training and media regulation and the effects it has on truth in our world. The game suggests that in response, one must reclaim the power to choose for oneself; you judge for yourself what it is that you want to pass on, something that a calculation-based computer program cannot do. As we continue to see more and more artificial intelligence in our lives, <i>Metal Gear Solid 2: Sons of Liberty</i> reminds us of the importance of maintaining the human aspect of personal judgement and choice. <p><h2>Solid Snake's Closing Speech</h2></p> <p><iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/lTGkPI4P6_0?si=u6tW7w7d4UaJ8jJp" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p> <p><h2>Works Cited:</h2></p> <p>Baudrillard, Jean.
