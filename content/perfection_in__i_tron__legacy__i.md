# Perfection in <i>Tron: Legacy</i>

        <p><i>Tron: Legacy</i> is a story that first introduces humans entering a digital world. Sam Flynn goes in looking for his father that has been missing for 20 years. The film takes place inside The Grid, a digital world created for AI programs. The story follows themes of existentialism and the search for perfection. We see these themes primarily through CLU (Codified Likeness Utility) , a digital copy of Kevin Flynn, the creator. CLU was created to build the perfect system, where perfection was mandatory and imperfection was eliminated. However, the film ultimately shows that this goal is flawed, because eliminating imperfection also eliminates the possibility for growth, change, and true progress.</p> <p>Clu is an artificial intelligence created for perfection, except this is impossible. Kevin’s idea of perfection is different from CLU’s, even though they both start with the same goal. For Clu, it’s binary, something is either perfect or it is not, and anything that does not fit must be removed. For Kevin, perfection is the failure and unpredictability of human nature. This difference is important because it shows that perfection is not an objective truth, but something that depends on perspective. CLU cannot understand this, and because of that, he becomes rigid and extreme.  These ideas closely resemble <i>Frankenstein</i> by Mary Shelley. Victor Frankenstein (the creator) and the creature (CLU) go against each other, fighting and trying to grasp their purpose. For the creature, he yearns for a mate, a female version of his own kind, binary.</p> <p><i>Tron: Legacy</i> brings up an important topic, the idea of perfection and how artificial intelligence evolves. For both Clu and the creature, they start by listening to their creator and following their commands. As they progress and seek knowledge through their own means, they come to realize how imperfect humans are. For Clu imperfection came as a humanistic nature. The creature saw imperfection in their behaviour, their hypocrisy and cruelty. “I am malicious because I am miserable. Am I not shunned and hated by all mankind?” (Shelley 145). The importance for this imperfection relates back to how Descartes’ error is separating mind and body. Humans are emotional beings, we are humans because of our imperfections. Clu and the creature find these imperfections in humans.</p> <p>Perfection can never be achieved, and both texts show that the attempt to reach it leads to negative consequences. Humans are designed to be flawed, and these flaws allow growth, emotion and individuality. Machines on the other hand are built to be perfect and precise. They are created to achieve what man cannot do, for Clu it is to create the perfect system, for the creature it was an attempt to create the perfect being. <i>Tron: Legacy</i> ultimately presents a digital frontier where humans are labeled as imperfect, yet it is exactly this imperfection that makes them capable of change. Artificial intelligence seeking the perfect system and going through tyranny to achieve it. Machines try to replicate humans, but they fail because they cannot fully grasp the value of imperfection.</p> <p><h2>Work Cited</h2></p> <p>Wood, Aylish. “Contests and Simulations: Tron: Legacy’s Connections with Technologies.” Journal of Film and Video, vol. 66, no. 3, 2014, pp. 31–42.
