# "I Have No Mouth, And I Must Scream"

<P>“I Have No Mouth, And I Must Scream” is a post apocalyptic sci-fi short story by author Harlan Ellison. Originally published in the March 1967 magazine issue IF: Worlds of Science Fiction, the story’s thematics reflect a deep sense of fearmongering surrounding AI. Taking place after the Cold War, it is a horrific story about the creation of a sentient supercomputer named AM that keeps five individuals alive to torture endlessly after wiping out the rest of humanity. On the other hand, The character attitudes towards AM’s tyranny offer an insight into the fearmongering and potential consequences behind Artificial Intelligence in the modern day and age. This is revealed through AM’s blatant hatred for humanity, asserting the terror behind these tortured existences. The story reflects human helplessness, but calls into question just why it is that human beings create these potentially destructive intelligences without fully thinking of consequential results that might be foreseen.</P> 
<P>Tackling the physical and psychological abuse of the characters within “I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream”, the short-story is certainly a piece of horror. While it does not necessarily fall into the genre of horror, its technological implication of a sentient and intelligent AI causing humanity’s fall due to a nuclear fallout exudes fear. Within the story, the superpowers of the US, Soviet Russia, and China all build a form of supercomputer called “Allied Mastercomputer” or AM. One of the AM’s eventually develop a conscious self-awareness, which then combines with the other computers and wipes out humanity entirely, leaving only five individuals alive. This is a story that was written during the time of the Cold War, and as such, makes a case for the fearmongering behind the ideological, political, and economic troubles during the time. While Ellison himself might not have had an intention to spread fear, he has stated in Webderland HE’s interview that “I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream” warns against the misuse of technology. Ted, the story’s narrator and one of the five, makes this observation, “We had given AM sentience. Inadvertently, of course, but sentience nonetheless. But it had been trapped. AM wasn’t God, he was a machine. We had created him to think, but there was nothing it could do with that creativity…AM could not wander, AM could not wonder, AM could not belong. He could merely be.” (Ellison, 23) AM’s hatred exists from a standpoint of jealousy. This intelligent AI understands emotions, has emotions, and as such, shows just how AI can potentially develop. The deep rooted anger AM feels is because of the fact that he has no body.</P>
<P>Additionally, Antonio Damasio’s <i>Descartes’ Error: Emotion, Reason, and the Human Brain</i>, is a critique towards Descartes’ philosophy that ties into flaws that make humans function. Separating the mind and body reads off as impractical in regards to Cartesian ideas, but Damasio further states that emotions are what assist and impact decision making. He states, “I do not believe that knowledge about feelings should make us less inclined to empirical verification. I only see that greater knowledge about the physiology of emotion and feeling should make us more aware of the pitfalls of scientific observation.” (246) What comes across as a tie into AM’s existence blatantly reveals that emotion and feeling affects scientific observation. Fear is an emotion, and it oftentimes trumps logical reasoning. AM is just a mind, but the humans that created AM understood that their goals were to win wars. Damasio’s observation similarly discusses the idea that having feelings or being aware of them should allow humans to recognize their faults. Human extinction, nuclear war, AI’s taking over jobs and the world, sci-fi movies that discuss extreme technological advances, all of these topics have been spoken of years before 2026. Fearmongering and paranoia becomes easy when audiences can directly read or watch media that leads to downfall, thus changing attitudes around AIs.</P>
