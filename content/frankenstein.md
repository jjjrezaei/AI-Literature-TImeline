# Frankenstein

Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein is an important addition to the timeline because it explores the question of what makes someone human. In the novel, Victor Frankenstein creates this being and ends up abandoning it as it develops consciousness. The creature, after being left alone, had to explore the world– the same as a child does, through observation, imitation, and experience. The creature watches a family to understand humans, he learns to read and learns language. This is the same process as humans go through when they are born and go through the stages of life– they learn from their surroundings and people. The creature explains in Vol 2, chapter 3 “by opening my eyes, as I now suppose, the light poured in upon me again. I walked, and, I believe, descended; but I presently found a great alteration in my sensations.” and “but I now found that I could wander on at liberty, with no obstacles which I could not either surmount or avoid” (Shelley 121). This mirrors the stages of development of human life– individuals are not born with this knowledge but obtain it through observation and interaction with others. It also shows that humanity doesn’t begin with physical appearance but with being self aware and through perception. This raises the deeper question of, if the Creature was able to develop awareness and consciousness just as humans do, what separates him from them? This relates to Ai now because, if Ai learns from humans, and talks and reacts the way we normally would, then what would differentiate us from them if the learning process is the same? In chapter 4 of vol 2, the creature, when talking about the De Lacey family, whom he observed, says  “when they were unhappy, I felt depressed; when they rejoiced, I sympathized in their joys” (Shelley 129). This shows how the creature was able to exhibit feelings and emotions. The creature got educated and learned to feel by watching humans, which is how humans learn as well and then he was able to feel emotions soon after. In Meissner’s text “Artificial intelligence: consciousness and conscience” they write “In human evolution, consciousness was a precondition for conscience. First, humans developed a consciousness, becoming aware of their existence and their actions, and why they performed them. Later, they developed a conscience telling them whether their actions were right or wrong. The development of a conscience strongly supported the survival of mankind”.  This quote recognizes that beings must first become self aware before they can fully understand actions and could judge what's right and wrong. In Damasio’s excerpt from Descartes’ Error, he writes “In the beginning it was being, and only later was it thinking. And for us now, as we come into the world and develop, we still begin with being, and only later do we think. We are, and then we think…” In Damasio’s quote, he lays out that the state of being comes first and from there thinking follows. This ties back to the creature because he becomes first— feels, sees, hears, and then he begins to think after. Overall, Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein should be included in the timeline because it challenges the idea that thinking alone makes someone human and instead suggests that being human stems from being consciousness, emotion, and conscience. So, if the Creature (in this case Ai now)  is similar to human beings, what truly differs them from us?
