# As We May Think

As We May Think written by Vannevar Bush is a foundational text that predicts the complex relationship between computers and humans, and the rise of artificial intelligence for a future where humans consult artificial intelligence more and more. In this article, Bush explores the possibility of advanced developments for various devices that keep records of the human experience- think film, photography, and vocal recorders. More specifically, Bush is concerned with said devices performing calculations and how humans program and input these calculations for production. This interest serves as a precursor for transactions between humans and artificial intelligence where humans look to AI to perform tasks and AI shortcuts "thinking" for humans.
Bush hints at the necessity for artificial intelligence to step in and perform minute computations which unexpectedly leads to more imaginative, abstract ideas being computed as well. He writes, "For mature thought there is no mechanical substitution. But creative thought and essentially repetitive thought are very different things. For the latter there are, and may be, powerful mechanical aids" (pg. 116). It can be assumed that what he meant by mature thought is creative in nature, a way humans process and perceive artistic matters. He does not consider a need to compute creativity but for the logistics that exist otherwise. Surprisingly, where AI could be used for mere calculations it is also being used to aid in creative thinking. What we view as artificial intelligence today is viewed in various ways, but not limited to ChatGPT- an innovative chat bot service used that can essentially be asked any question. There is concern that increased usage leads to dependency, which leads to diminished independent creativity. Researchers find, "Some students appeared to rely on GPT Chat as the sole source of information in their learning process, without considering critical analysis of the information provided. This study also found that some students struggled with divergent thinking, which is the ability to generate new and original ideas" (Melinda, Rohmah et.al 39). This is significant since Bush has a seemingly bright proposal when it comes to tedious mental labor, but doesn't seem to grasp the possibility of all sorts of mental work becoming tedious where AI can assist. Although this specific development was unprecedented via Bush's work, he still acknowleges a need for efficiency that speeds up the "thinking process" that humans do.
Bush does try to draw a line at computation and indexing though usage of artificial intelligence goes beyond that. He offers, "The human mind does not work that way. It operates by association. With one item in its grasp, it snaps instantly to the next that is suggested by the association of thoughts. Man cannot hope fully duplicate this mental process artificially, but he certainly ought to be able to learn from it" (page 121). Here, he sort of pokes at both a limitation and uniqueness in human thought. What he means by indexing is organizational, mechanical, orderly but humans think more spotty, non-linear, and associative. While it appears that thinking that way would be revered next to the rigidity of computation, it could actually call for its necessity. He goes on to say, "Consider a future device for individual use, which is sort of a mechanized private file and library… a memex is a device in which an individual stores all his books, records, and communications " (pg 121). Is the concept of the memex not just what we know today as digital computers and even our cell phones? Both individualized and storing what is personal to us. Artificial intelligence could not exist if not for the storage that came with digital computers, and it seems Bush is the first to coin these progressions.
Source: Rohmah, Mutia Mawadah, and Dasri Rati. "THE EFFECT OF USING CHAT GPT ON STUDENTS CREATIVITY LEARNING." Mandeh: Jurnal Pendidikan Islam 1.1 (2024): 32-41.
