# Launch of the iPhone

<p>Phones used to have one purpose, that was to make phone calls. In 2007 phones were undergoing a slow transformation.  The cellphones were generally flip phones with mostly terrible cameras and awkward keyboards. The company Apple was already making waves with the iPod that had become the de facto MP3 player, when Steve Jobs announced the iPhone on January 9, 2007, he was the herald of the new era. On June 29, 2007, The iPhone released and the era of the smartphone began and changed the way humans interacted with technology and each other in unprecedented ways.<p/>
The concept of a smartphone is not drastically different from personal computer, which had become common household items even for many lower-class Americans.  What the iPhone changed was how many things it could do, how many tasks it could simplify or make better with Apps.  The iPhone accomplished a “man computer symbiosis” as Licklider predicted, with Apps becoming the “humanly extended machines”.  Most of the first apps were replacements for things we had used either pen and paper for such as a calendar.  The smartphones replaced alarm clocks and watches slowly.  In the [[Licklider]] text they mention that many tasks that computers were capable of doing would take too long to be practical in a real scenario such as a hypothetical battle. Smartphones shorten the time to complete a task even more than PC’s just for the fact that they are handheld.

<p>Vannevar Bush predicted a device called the memex short for memory extender that would be able to remember information that a normal human could never be able to accomplish.  His prediction for this device was a massive desktop sized computer.  The iPhone at launch was not entirely capable of doing every invention from “As We May Think” article but with updates and upgrades all of the predictions would come true on a single device.  The cyclops camera while not worn on the head was represented by a 2 Megapixel camera. The Vocoder feature would not arrive until updates were made but would eventually include voice accessibility features. Microfilm caches became less popular over time; however, a similar concept would develop with e-books that would be available through the app store. The iPhone had a simple calculator-built in. As for the Memex itself, Bush’s most ambitious idea? The spirit of the memex lives on through the internet, and the iPhone, and almost all smartphones from this point on would have a browser that would let users access the World Wide Web to realize the dream of unlimited information.  No, the iPhone did not invent the internet, but it made it accessible to a new generation of users.<p/><p> The smartphone era had begun for those few in the United States, that could afford it.  The iPhone launched at 499 to 599 USD, which adjusted for inflation from 2007 to 2026 is roughly equivalent to 786 to 944 USD.  This hefty price for such a small device presented a barrier for those not willing or able to make the investment. The United States was the first to see the launch of the iPhone followed by Europe then Asia in the following year 2008.  Apple would sell over four million iPhones in the first year (Reuters,2008).  Competitors would come in afterwards with the HTC dream built on the android Operating system.<p><p> Predictions from the past began to come true as Steve Jobs made Licklider, and Bush’s assertions to be prophetic.
