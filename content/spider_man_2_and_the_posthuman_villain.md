# Spider-Man 2 and the Posthuman Villain

<p>Exploring <i>Katherine Hayles</i> and her book <i>How we became Posthuman</i> has changed the way we see human and machine interaction. The common notion was that humans and machines were separate, there was a disconnect between both. Hayles deconstructs this idea and creates the term “Posthuman”, a version of us that has adapted to the technology we consume. “This realization… is so broad in its effects and so deep in its consequences that it is transforming the liberal subject, regarded as the model of the human since the Enlightenment, into the posthuman.” (Hayles xiv). The idea of a Posthuman is scary, but we’ve been in this state for years now. The introduction of the smart phone pushes us further into the Posthuman, technology is getting more immersive and intertwined with our day-to-day lives. Though there hasn’t been any “real” harm to the Posthuman we are going to explore a character that has mutated into something different. When the information being shared between human and machine becomes mutated and corrupted the result is an overpowering of one entity over the other, a power imbalance that affects the Posthuman.</p> <p>Dr Otto Octavius from the movie <i>Spider-Man 2</i> is an example of Hayles’ Posthuman. Octavius creates a set of “smart arms” that help him control the high intensity heat of his experiment. These arms are controlled by him through a neural link connected to his brain and his spine, the arms receive the information sent from the sender. This language and process supports Hayles’ information theory, the important element of the smart arms is not the information that is being sent, rather the way that the information is being transmitted. Octavius and the arms have become one, they’re in a symbiotic relationship, they became the Posthuman. The idea of the Posthuman is how technology is embedded into humans, how together we’ve become a type of cyborg. This idea is only possible when both human and machine coexist, but what we see in <i>Spider-Man 2</i> is how this new entity can become a concern. The villain that we see in the film can be considered a cautionary tale for the Posthuman. An entity that mutates and essentially gets overpowered by one party. Octavius had implanted an “inhibitor chip” which would allow him to stay in control of the arms. This also connects back to Shelly’s <i>Frankenstein</i> and how the creator loses control of the creation. What we see is the Posthuman losing control and mutating the information that is being relayed to the arms.</p> <p>Hayles’ Posthuman is the future for human history and existence, we rely so much on technology and machines for our information and existence. However, if we are not cautious and careful of how we interact with machines the balance breaks and mutates. The information that is relayed becomes randomized that the mutation and randomness “marks a rupture of pattern so extreme that the expectation of continuous replication can no longer be sustained.” (Hayles 33).</p> <p><h2>Work Cited</h2></p> <p>Hayles, N. Katherine. <i>How We Became Posthuman: Virtual Bodies in Cybernetics, Literature, and Informatics.</i> University of Chicago Press, 1999.
