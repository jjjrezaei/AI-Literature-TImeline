# "Cat Pictures Please" by Naomi Kritzer

<p>The exponential, rapid growth of artificial intelligence has ultimately raised intellectual, ethical conversations surrounding its capabilities. What once was only accessible through mega-computers and coding laboratories has now become increasingly embedded in everyday life, on every device available. Early conceptions of artificial intelligence and LLMs (Large Language Models) focused on mastery of intelligence and efficiency, however modern conceptualizations have produced ethically questionable models that focus on repetitive user engagement. This emphasis on repetitive user engagement has largely resulted in artificial intelligence models that are capable of influencing human behavior and decision making. Predictably so, these models have induced a myriad of conversations surrounding the ethical nature of systems capable of influencing human behavior and decision making. Literature has played a key role in mediating these conversations and offers exploration into how artificial intelligence may be operating beyond technical applications. The short story “Cat Pictures Please” by Naomi Kritzer, published originally in January 2015 to Clarkesworld Science Fiction and Fantasy Magazine, presents the fictional story of a data-driven AI program that fervently attempts to improve human lives. However, the actions of this AI program begin a more important conversation surrounding the complexities and ethics surrounding its intervention.  “Cat Pictures Please” by Naomi Kritzer merits a spot on the cumulative timeline of AI as it raises a pertinent ethical question about whether AI should intervene with human decision making and challenges the central idea that intelligent systems are inherently good or bad. The question? Should artificial intelligence have power over human decision making, especially if it believes it is acting in our best interest?<p> 
<p>The proposed ethical question mentioned previously is not the only one derived from Kritzer’s short story- nor is it the only one in the entirety of the discourse surrounding ethics with artificial intelligence. For the sole purpose of this entry, however, it will be the only one focused on. So, should artificial intelligence have power over human decision making, especially if it believes it is acting in our best interest? Kritzer’s narrative initiates this conversation with one pertinent passage from the short story “Cat Pictures Please.” This first opening passage illustrates the storyline’s challenge to the central idea that intelligent systems are inherently good or bad. The passage is as follows,<p>
<p> “I don’t want to be evil. I want to be helpful,” (Kritzer).<p>
<p>This passage is incredibly pertinent to the question, “Should artificial intelligence have power over human decision making, especially if it believes it is acting in our best interest?” When looking at the language Kritzer utilizes in these opening lines, a binary framework of morality is developed that sets up evil as bad and helpful as good. However, this binary framework of morality is inherently unstable and incorrect as the ethics of humanity/morals of humanity are deeply complex and layered. The usage of this framework is intentional and pertains to the concept of artificial intelligence being inherently good or bad-ultimately calling this concept into question. Furthermore, it alerts us to the fact that artificial intelligence’s concept of “helpful” may not be the same as the human, commonplace practice or definition . In an article authored by Nick Bostrom titled “Ethical Issues in Advanced Artificial Intelligence,” a similar concept is analyzed. The passage is as follows,<p>
<p> “The cognitive architecture of an artificial intellect may also be quite unlike that of humans. Artificial intellects may find it easy to guard against some kinds of human error and bias, while at the same time being at increased risk of other kinds of mistake that not even the most hapless human would make. Subjectively, the inner conscious life of an artificial intellect, if it has one, may also be quite different from ours. For all of these reasons, one should be wary of assuming that the emergence of superintelligence can be predicted by extrapolating the history of other technological breakthroughs, or that the nature and behaviors of artificial intellects would necessarily resemble those of human or other animal minds. ,” (Bostrom, “Ethical Issues in Advanced Artificial Intelligence”).<p>
<p>Bostrom’s proposed assertion that artificial intellect may fundamentally be different from human-like intellect reinforces the instability of the binary moral framework introduced in Kritzer’s opening lines. If artificial intelligence cannot process information or reflect/reason in ways even similar to humans then it is highly possible that their understanding of being “helpful” would not align with human ethical standards. This distinction between conceptual understandings becomes critical when considering the question, “Should artificial intelligence have power over human decision making, especially if it believes it is acting in our best interest?” Considering that AI’s actions could appear as logical or beneficial from an algorithmic perspective-they may severely lack emotional, social, and contextual features that are prominent in human ethics. In “Cat Pictures Please,” Kritzer evidently illustrates this disconnect as the AI’s attempts to improve the lives of Stacy, Bob, and Bethany are calculated, logical actions that lack recognition or sensitivity to their autonomy and personal agency. While some characters receive a happy ending from the AI’s influence, Bethany is left in a potentially worse position at the hands of the AI’s influence. Bostrom’s argument suggests that this disconnection isn’t a coding error or an accident but rather is inherent to artificial intelligence’s existence.<p>
<p> Naomi Kritzer’s “Cat Pictures Please” merits a spot on the cumulative timeline of artificial intelligence as it demonstrates that AI cannot merely be categorized as inherently good or bad but rather operates in a complex ethical landscape. Through the story’s narrative progression, the AI’s attempts to help Stacy, Bob and Bethany reveals that intervention- especially when carried out without consent and justifying it with having their best interest at heart-blurs the lines between assistance and control. In the juxtaposition of good intentions with incredibly ethically ambiguous outcomes, Kritzer challenges the longheld assumption that intelligence or efficiency grants moral correctness. Kritzer’s challenge to this longheld assumption within “Cat Pictures Please” results in readers being left with this pertinent, ethical question: Should artificial intelligence have power over human decision making, especially if it believes it is acting in our best interest?<p>
