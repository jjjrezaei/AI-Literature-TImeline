# SDSU's AI Major Announcement 

On September 11, 2025, SDSU announced that they were introducing a new major titled “Artificial Intelligence and Human Responsibility (BS)”. This announcement came largely to the surprise of none, as AI has been pushed by SDSU in late 2025. In H.G. Wells The Island of Dr. Moreau, questions of science and ethics present itself to be the main premise of the plot. Pendrick reflects upon Dr. Moreau’s methods by claiming, “It was not the first time that conscience has turned against the methods of research.” As we learn later throughout the novel, the Doctor largely turns a blind eye towards ethics and encounters the darker side of science by ignoring those concerns. 

By introducing an AI major, SDSU claims its new degree equips you with the knowledge and skills to understand and evaluate AI technologies in an ethical and sustainable way. However, this major does not focus on taking many classes focused on sustainability or rhetoric and instead focuses heavily on geology classes as prerequisites. On the embedded hyperlink, the major claims to help students “investigate not only how AI works, but when, why, and whether it should be deployed.” An intriguing argument, it invites students to explore a new world of AI, one free from the stigma of its initial use. The text presents a cheery and positive tone, further luring the audience into complacency. Even the interface of the Canvas email itself serves to normalize the use of AI within the education sector. It does so by presenting the adoption of AI as inevitable and uncontroversial, subtly encouraging students to accept its integration into academic life without question. The email does more than inform—it persuades. This email is relevant, as it leads to future effects within not just the educational sector, but the job sector as well. San Diego State’s adherence to pushing AI into the educational sphere mirrors a dynamic present within Wells’ novel: when science pushes past ethical boundaries faster than we can process them.

When respected institutions introduce and promote emerging technologies, these tools are accepted as credible, beneficial, and crucial to the success of staff and students with little to no pushback. Institutional endorsement discourages skepticism, leading to less critical analysis of the implications of the tools promoted. With the promotion of AI in education, its uncritical acceptance reflects the central warning presented within The Island of Dr. Moreau, where scientific progress is pursued in place of ethical responsibility. Rather than fostering thoughtful reflection, the rapid push of an AI major within an academic space substitutes the focus on embracing and funding established majors already present at SDSU. The embracing of AI mirrors Moreau’s unchecked power, showcasing the hazards of pushing for modernity and accepting technological advancement without fully understanding, caring, or taking responsibility for its broader implications.
