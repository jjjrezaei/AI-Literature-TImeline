# No Manifesto- Yvonne Rainer

<p>In 1965, Yvonne Rainer created the “No Manifesto” as a choreographic approach to dancing. It questioned what dance and performance could look like by insisting on a list of “no”s that the dancers had to follow. This could result in set choreography or could be used as guidelines to improvise, also called a “score”. As a postmodern dance term, a “score” operates like the rules to a game; the guidelines to the quality of movement, use of space, and use of time. Someone who tries to dance following this manifesto would find it impossible, yet the act of trying is generative. The simultaneity of impossibility and research towards a set ideal is reminiscent of Jean Baudrillard’s Simulacra and Simulation. In it, he questions how striving towards a fantasy world is an attempt at simulation. He writes,</p>
<p>“Disneyland is a perfect model of all the entangled orders of simulacra. It is first of all a play of illusions and phantasms: the Pirates, the Frontier, the Future World, etc. This imaginary world is supposed to ensure the success of the operation. But what attracts the crowds the most is without a doubt the social microcosm, the religious, miniaturized pleasure of real America, of its constraints and joys. (12).”</p>
<p>Since our lives can be built around the premise of achievement of these ideals, Boudrillard concludes that our reality is the “dream world” and the simulation is the world we are living in. To attempt to dance to Rainer’s stipulations is to simulate attempts at an equally fantastical reality, despite her saying “No to transformations and magic and make-believe.”.</p>

<p>In the interest of defining artificial intelligence and chronicling its literary and historical importance on our timeline, Rainer’s “no manifesto” is an example that our bodies are research-ful. Approaching this through “dancing” (movement, performance, etc.) is a reminder that research is ephemeral, embodied, and generative, regardless of medium or archival state. By placing a limit on the possibility of movement, especially with “No to moving or being moved”, Rainer reminds us that movement can be considered anything, used for any purpose, and be done at any time. All it requires is the will to start. Dancing can use but does not require a lab, technology, or prior knowledge to research it. The body is the archive, the archivist, the researcher and the audience. Likewise, there is no body inaccessible nor person incapable of dancing except for the one who refuses to try.</p>

<p>Dance, as a method of research, offers a perspective unique to writing or typing because it doesn’t require a medium or archive other than one’s body to make it happen. It’s more like brainstorming, or conversing. The flow of ideas doesn’t get saved unless there is another element present archiving it, like a video, notation, or other person’s memory. One’s own memory, interests, and environment guide the state of the research and how they remember it. The researcher’s focus is split between the subject they are investigating and navigating their environment. The addition of machines for notation, video, writing etc. add another navigational step. Our bodies are the first machine we investigate with, though our attention may jump from how the fingers type to what they are typing. Boudrillard writes about this cycle of inspiration and how simulation produces and is produced by simulacra. He states,</p>
<p>“Simulation, on the contrary, stems from the utopia of the principle of equivalence.from the radical negation of the sign as value, from the sign as the reversion and death sentence of every reference. Whereas representation attempts to absorb simulation by interpreting it as a false representation, simulation envelops the whole edifice of representation itself as a simulacrum.</p>
<p>Such would be the successive phases of the image:</p>
<p>it is the reflection of a profound reality;</p>
<p>it masks and denatures a profound reality;</p>
<p>it masks the absence of a profound reality;</p>
<p>it has no relation to any reality whatsoever: it is its own pure Simulacrum (6).”</p>
<p>Attempts to reproduce the desired reality, whether that be a fantasy world or a dance score, result in the creation of simulacra, which then inspire new ways in. Something is made of something (medium), says something about what it is made of (message), obscures what it was made of (archive and reception), and then stands on its own.</p>

<p>Take this example of me trying to dance according to the “no manifesto”. I begin moving. This movement happens to look like me raising both arms to my sides slowly, over and over again. During this, I re-evaluate my movement over and over again to see if it fits the score, and I also forget about it. Now,  I question the impossibility of it and have stopped and started over and over again due to my attention span. My unfaithfulness is creating a new reality, and rejects the score I was attempting. Then, in the absence of the original, the copy becomes the artifact: I am raising my arms up and down. If I chose to research this action as the score, the cycle would start again. There is a great chance that knowledge gained will be lost as one’s attention shifts, but that is a sacrifice necessary for the ability to continue. Our memories don’t have endless storage, yet this begets the gift of rediscovery.</p>

<p>Rainer questions the meanings of authenticity, performance, and pedestrian. For the lens of AI, it is a reminder that our bodies, ourselves, can be tools to research. There are so many ways our bodies are used as tools for the purpose of capital: jobs, algorithms, website cookies, completing homework or other nefarious means of surveillance. We should be reminded that our bodies are our tools for living, being, dancing, and trying. Boudrillard’s cycle of simulacra and simulation is always happening as we navigate our world. Valence is a tool we all possess and should use, lest we be cuckold to our own lives and let other people or machines be the only things that analyze ourselves. I encourage everyone to try this out, as a score, or any prompt you can come up with. Dancing to music could be the score, and would be a great place to start, but remember that dancing can start anywhere with no requirements. There is no body incapable of dance except for the one who refuses to try.</p>
