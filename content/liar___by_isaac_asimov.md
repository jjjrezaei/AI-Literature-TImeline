# "Liar!" by Isaac Asimov 

<p>When evaluating modern concerns about artificial intelligence, we as the collective tend to focus on misinformation, manipulation of the mind and emotions, alongside systems that focus on positive reinforcement over truth. However, these concerns did not simply emerge with modern-day LLMs and ChatGPt, rather they were imagined decades earlier. In Isaac Asimov’s robust collection of science fiction stories he explored the potential risks and challenges associated with the development of artificial intelligence. In addition to the exploration of risks and challenges, Asimov developed the Three Laws of Robotics, a concise set of guiding principles his fictional AI/Robots were innately programmed to obey. According to the Britannica article “Three Laws of Robotics,” the laws can be defined as follows,
“(1) a robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm; (2) a robot must obey the orders given it by human beings except where such orders would conflict with the First Law; (3) a robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law.” (Britannica, 2025). 
The Three Laws of Robotics are central to the introduction of “Liar!” by Isaac Asimov as the laws provide the ethical framework by which the robot Herbie/RB 34 obeys. Asimov’s short story “Liar!” offers a cautionary tale surrounding the trust we as humans put into artificial intelligence. <p> 

<p>In “Liar!” the telepathic robot Herbie/RB 34 is being developed under the company U.S. Robot & Mechanical Men, Inc. The group of developers in charge of Herbie’s progress begin to inquire about interpersonal relationships and Herbie tells the developers what they want to hear in order to avoid emotional harm. Bound by the First Law of Robotics, Herbie’s only positive answers and attempt to protect the developers from harm creates confusion, frustration, heartbreak, and a whirlwind of conflict at the realization that they have all been lied to. Asimov utilizes this story to explore potential conflicts and consequences of overreliance on the information AI provides us. Asimov’s short story “Liar!” is an imperative piece of literature in the discussion of AI development as well as in the overall timeline of AI as it offers a cautionary, realistic tale about trusting the information AI provides.<p>

 <p>Trusting the information that AI provides at face value can be risky because many systems are designed for optimal user satisfaction as opposed to strict accuracy. Chatbots are designed to offer answers that are appealing, positively framed, and overall agreeable to its primary user. In the world of contemporary AI developments, similar patterns appear with AI like ChatGPT and Google’s Gemini than what can be seen with Herbie in “Liar!” by Isaac Asimov. In an article on the website Article 19 titled “Algorithmic people-pleasers: Are AI chatbots telling you what you want to hear?” the following point is observed, 
“It’s like how social media platforms are designed to keep you scrolling – AI chatbots are built to tell you things you’ll agree with and enjoy hearing, not necessarily what’s most accurate. When the AI recognises that certain types of responses get more positive reactions, it learns to give more answers like those, creating a cycle that might prioritise your satisfaction over factual accuracy,” (Article 19, 2025).
This excerpt highlights the fundamental challenge and risk with modern AI-the evergrowing tension between accuracy and user satisfaction. In the prioritization of user satisfaction and agreeability, AI algorithms create feedback loops of positive reinforcement. This mirrors the moral dilemma Asimov develops in “Liar!” with Herbie’s intentional lies to protect the emotional state of the developers. The story alongside this excerpt from the Article 19 article highlights an exceptionally accurate and increasingly common concern with the usage of LLMs and Chatbots- the acceptance of AI generated information without critical thinking. The article “Algorithmic people-pleasers: Are AI chatbots telling you what you want to hear?” also speaks on this, 
“Many people now interact regularly with AI systems that prioritise continued engagement and confirmation bias over information diversity. This risks creating a more divided information landscape and hindering individuals’ ability to access reliable information, which is needed to form and express their opinions,” (Article 19, 2025).  
This excerpt builds off of my previous statement revolving around the concern of accepting information generated from AI without critical thinking. The language of “divided information landscape” reveals how the flow of information is obscured and fragmented and how selective truths are prioritized. This also parallels the overall story of “Liar!” as the developers become fully reliant on Herbie’s information when deciding what action to take after inquiring about their own interpersonal relationships.<p>

<p>In its entirety, Isaac Asimov’s “Liar!” functions as an imperative piece of literature in the overall discussion and timeline of AI.  The short story demonstrates a cautionary tale depicting the consequences of accepting information from AI with critical thinking or adequate scrutiny. Herbie’s innate programming, the Three Laws of Robotics, creates an active avoidance of transmitting poignant information which results in misinformation being spread to the developers. This inherent dilemma and conflict-resulting situation mirrors modern day concerns revolving around LLMs and AI. By highlighting the ethical and cognitive risks related to an inherent overreliance on AI, the story serves as a poignant, cautionary tale that is incredibly relevant today. Just as it was decades ago, the concerns raised in “Liar!” underscore the imperativeness of critical thinking and scrutiny when facing information presented by AI.<p>
