# Lego Ninjago: AI Brotherhood

<p>Zane from the 2012 children’s cartoon “Lego Ninjago: Masters of Spinjitsu,” shows how AIs can be used to reveal and understand human characteristics as a literary tool to help young children learn to accept people’s differences, a unique and optimistic twist on Frankenstein's monster. </p><p>Zane is one of the four protagonists, characterized early in the series by his seeming inability to fit in with the others. Zane is unable to resonate with their humor and is shown to have a general lack of understanding of social norms as showcased when he wears a pink apron and is teased by the other ninjas. Not only does he not understand why the pink apron warrants teasing, he also doesn’t understand why the others find it to be funny. </p><p>In episode two: “Home,” Kai tells Sensei Wu, “We like the guy. He's really smart. He's just... a little off sometimes,” (Lego Ninjago: Masters of Spinjitsu, S1E2). To which Sensei Wu responds: “Zane is a brother and brothers are often different,” (Lego Ninjago: Masters of Spinjitsu, S1E2). The subtext within this interaction highlights how Zane is seemingly portrayed as having many characteristics of autism, showing how Zane is meant to be a gateway into beginning to understand autism for young children. Zane is referred to like this by the other Ninjas multiple times, highlighting how he is inherently different from his friends, although Kai’s optimistic tone phrases Zane's strangeness as not necessarily being bad. Sensei Wu’s response further helps to normalize Zane’s differences, to help the other Ninja accept their friend despite his quirks. This episode portrays a valuable lesson for young children who may not understand why a friend, sibling, or classmate with autism may act differently, as well as learn to not tease or mock them, but to accept them for their differences. </p><p>The big twist of the season comes in episode seven “Tick Tock,” in which Zane uncovers and comes to terms with his past, discovering in the process that he is an android. Zane had already been embraced by the Ninjas for his differences, so after learning the shocking truth about why Zane is different, the Ninjas don’t think twice about Zane as they continue to embrace him in their team. The acceptance of Zane is a unique twist on Frankenstein's monster, rather than facing ostracization and abandonment like the monster, Zane is shown brotherhood from the ninjas and love from his creator or father. Zane’s eventual heroic sacrifice and return from the dead in the third and fourth seasons, cast him as a Christ-like figure, mirroring the monster’s fallen angel persona he associates himself with. </p><p>Zane showcases a unique use of AI in literature, in that his is a window into understanding human behavior and promoting empathy and acceptance of those who are different, strange, or weird. Zane is a remarkably bright twist on Frankenstein’s monster, that highlights the mistakes Victor made, leaving us only to imagine a world where the monster was treated with the same kindness as Zane. </p><p>“Lego Ninjago: Masters of Spinjitsu” season 1, episodes 2 and 7, Kevin and Dan Hageman, The Lego Group, 2012 </p><p>“Frankenstein”, Mary Shelly, 1818
