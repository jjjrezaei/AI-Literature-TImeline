# PLUR1BUS

<P>Apple TV’s <i>Pluribus</i> (sometimes styled PLUR1BUS) is a 2025 post-apocalyptic sci-fi drama series created by Vince Gilligan. The series follows actress Rhea Seahorn’s character, Carol Sturka, an author who is one of thirteen other individuals who are immune to an extraterrestrial virus that has since caused the rest of humanity to turn into a singular hivemind. The hivemind is dubbed as the “Others,” and come across as extremely peaceful and accommodating to those immune. They admit that they ultimately wish to seek out a way to assimilate the rest of those who are immune into the hivemind, which Carol is extremely against as a realistic and pessimistic person who values her individuality. The series itself can be seen as an analogy towards the integration of artificial intelligence in daily life. There are parallels to the lure of modern AI, and the thematics remain reminiscent of the idea that human nature continues to become increasingly comfortable with utilizing AI in ways that lead to its anthropomorphization. The “Others” reflect this idea towards the shift of suddenly accepting AI within our society without critically thinking about the potential psychological consequences of being compelled to speak to something familiar yet non-human, thus leading to the anthropomorphization of AI alongside human compliance.</P>
<P><i>Pluribus</i> begins with scientists discovering an extraterrestrial signal that is attempting to be deciphered. The virus is introduced into the world after an accident occurs in the main laboratory, and it is soon spread as an airborne pathogen when the United States military utilizes planes. Carol is caught in the midst of this after heading to a bar with her wife Helen, as the individuals around Carol, including her wife, begin to seize violently. She finds out roughly a day after Helen passes away that the large scale event is referred to as the “Joining,” where humanity has since become a hivemind. Eventually, every immune individual is sent a chaperone, a person (or multiple individuals) who accommodates to their needs. In Episode 2, “Pirate Lady,” Carol is in the midst of grieving the death of her wife when the “Others” send Zosia, an infected individual who looks extremely similar to a character in Carol’s novel. Every individual in the world apart of the “Others” shares the memories, talents, and knowledge within them, and despite this, remain fully peaceful and willing to following the instructions of those immune. Throughout multiple twists and revelations, Carol begins to find herself sexually attracted to Zosia despite understanding that Zosia is not an individual mind or person. She understands that they are attempting to assimilate her still, yet cannot help starting a brief relationship with Zosia after thinking she “understands” Carol’s feelings.</P>

Carol’s situation felt reflective of Joseph [[Weizenbaum]]’s observations on human interaction with the ELIZA chatbot. In <i>Inventing Eliza</i>, the authors recount, “After seeing its public reception, Weizenbaum was startled by the quick and often emotional attachments people would form with ELIZA…The tendency to attribute empathy and invest private feelings into a computer puzzled Weizenbaum.” (25-26) This passage directly exhibits the idea that Weizenbaum felt shocked that many of those who interacted with [[ELIZA]] found themselves being fully open with something non-human. It is acknowledged that ELIZA is a computer. There is no brain nor a body, yet people were openly sharing highly personal tidbits and felt a deep connection to a conversational partner. ELIZA was essentially thought of as anthropomorphized by the public. She is given human emotions and intentions for a program that is essentially scraping what is said in order to extend conversation. The immune humans of <i>Pluribus</i> exhibit behaviors that are extremely compliant of the hivemind, leading hedonistic lifestyles because they are able to or bizarrely accepting them because they take the faces of loved ones. The commodification of AI is easily used to often show how good life can be when it is stripped of deeper complexity. <i>Pluribus</i> reveals that many of the immune do not wish to live in a world that contains struggle at the promise of utopia. To those having interacted with ELIZA, AI, and the “Others,” these programs and beings are taken as mostly harmless and good willed, and are advertised as such despite the fact that certain individuals think and try to explain otherwise, like Carol and Weizenbaum.
