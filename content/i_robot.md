# I-Robot 

The film I-Robot directed by Alex Proyas is a pivotal piece of media that left a mark on me at a young age because of the way artificial intelligence was shown- through a lens of fear mongering via total integration into society. The largest takeaway is that while AI can be made palatable if you give it a host (robots in this case), it is still only replication at it's best. I-Robot's onscreen protagonist, Detective Spooner , identifies the 'cog in the machine' and the clear distinction between humans and artificial intelligence. There's a scene where a robot, Sonny, questions the detective about his blatant distrust towards him (and other robots). In which Detective Spooner states, "Human beings have dreams. Even dogs have dreams, but not you, you are just a machine. An imitation of life." While Sonny appears to be very pleasant, Spooner calls out the fact that he is programmed to be that way. Based on the original work I-Robot by Eando Binder, the movie depicts artificial intelligence in the form of humanistic robots that become helping hands among the human race. Robots can be seen fulfilling all sorts of service positions and even belonging to families/households to serve long-term. Citizens can purchase robots and those purchases are a spectacle and celebration for some, but evidently not for all. Our protagonist is deeply wary of and resentful towards the robots- seemingly due to the limitations of the concept of machinery. The contrast of Detective Spooner's attitude towards robots solidified the dystopian energy, and isn't an attitude lost in today's world as we adjust to modern AI.We know AI as software in our phones and social media apps, and developments such as Meta AI are becoming widespread and becoming more noticeable in online experiences every day. Just the integration of Meta AI popping up in search engines has consumers expressing frustration and disdain, mainly due to the values of its owners and and not having the choice to easily opt out of using the tool. Modern AI usage accompanied with a proud and/or careless attitude has stigma due to a misalignment in values. Those against artificial intelligence come from a perspective that values human creativity, tenacity, and dedication towards intelligence. These attitudes connect to some concepts expressed in Damasio's Descartes' Error: Emotion, Reason, and the Human Brain. Damasio critiques the ideas of the mind and the body being separate offered by early philosopher Descartes. Damasio explores just how interwoven reasoning and feelings are. Damasio offers, "Perhaps the most indispensable thing we can do as human beings, every day of our lives, is remind ourselves and others of our complexity, fragility, finiteness, and uniqueness" (pg 252). This speaks to the imperfect components that differentiate humans from artificial intelligence. Computational systems (machines) are seeking perfectionism through rigidly trained algorithms but, human intelligence and creations that come from humans are innately layered, sensitive, and indeed finite. Artificial intelligence mirrors human reasoning and logic but cannot be it, no matter how close it comes, because humanity is a uniquely shared experience. I-Robot demonstrates AI mimicking humanity to the extent it takes on mechanical bodies, even having some robots appear more helpful and bright than others (Sonny) but by the end of the film machineryis finally become regulated and policed due to a failed uprising. This raises questions as to not only how, but when AI will be heavily regulated
