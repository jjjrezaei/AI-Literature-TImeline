# The Mechanical Turk (1770)

<p>In 1770, a Hungarian inventor named Wolfgang von Kempelen introduced a machine called the Mechanical Turk. It appeared to be a chess-playing automaton, a machine that appeared to think and make decisions on its own. The device was built as a wooden cabinet filled with gears, with a human-sized figure dressed in Turkish clothing sitting on top. During public demonstrations, the machine played chess against human opponents and often won. It reportedly played against well-known figures such as Benjamin Franklin and Napoleon Bonaparte, which made many people believe that it was a true thinking machine.</p> <p>In reality, the Mechanical Turk was not intelligent at all. It was a carefully designed illusion. A human chess master was hidden inside the cabinet and controlled the machine’s movements from within. The interior of the cabinet was arranged to mislead audiences. When doors were opened to display the machinery, the hidden operator remained out of sight. As historian Tom Standage explains, the Mechanical Turk blurred the line between human and machine intelligence (Standage, 2002). Even though it was a hoax, the device is still important for understanding the history of artificial intelligence because it shows how easily people can believe that a machine is thinking.</p> <p><strong>The Mechanical Turk can also be connected to ideas about artificial life in Mary Shelley’s <em>Frankenstein</em> (1818). In the novel, Victor Frankenstein wants to create life and push beyond normal human limits. He says, “life and death appeared to me ideal bounds, which I should first break through, and pour a torrent of light into our dark world” (Shelley, 80). This shows that people were already thinking about creating artificial life long before modern technology. Like the Turk, Frankenstein’s creation challenges the line between human and non-human. However, the Mechanical Turk only created the illusion of intelligence, while Frankenstein’s creature is actually able to think and feel. This comparison shows that questions about artificial intelligence and what it means to think have existed for a long time.</strong></p> <p>The Mechanical Turk was part of a broader fascination with automata in the eighteenth century. These machines could imitate living actions such as writing, drawing, or playing music. However, most automata only copied physical movement. The Turk was different because it appeared to demonstrate intelligence. It seemed to plan moves, respond to its opponent, and play a complex strategic game. This created the impression that a machine could possess a mind, not just a body.</p> <p>The story of the Mechanical Turk also connects to modern forms of artificial intelligence. Today, many AI systems are presented as fully automated, but they often depend on human labor behind the scenes. For example, Amazon Mechanical Turk, which is named after this historical machine, allows companies to hire people to complete small tasks that help train AI systems. As researchers Mary L. Gray and Siddharth Suri argue, this hidden human labor, which they call ghost work, is essential but largely invisible (Gray and Suri, 2019). Like the original Turk, modern AI systems can hide the human effort that makes them function.</p> <p>Including the Mechanical Turk in an AI timeline challenges the idea that artificial intelligence is a recent invention that developed through steady technological progress. The Turk shows that the desire to create thinking machines existed long before computers. It also shows that AI has always involved illusion and performance. The machine did not actually think, but it convinced people that it did. This suggests that belief has always been an important part of how AI works.</p> <p>This example changes how we understand artificial intelligence. Instead of seeing AI as something entirely new, we can see it as part of a longer history of human imagination and interpretation. The Mechanical Turk reveals that intelligence in machines has often been staged or constructed rather than real. It also highlights the role of hidden labor, both in the past and in the present.</p> <p>The Mechanical Turk is significant because it shows that artificial intelligence is not only a technical achievement. It is also shaped by how people define intelligence and what they choose to believe about machines. By including it in the timeline, we can better understand AI as a cultural and historical phenomenon, not just a modern technological development.</p> <p><strong>Works Cited:</strong></p> <p><strong>Shelley, Mary. <em>Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus</em>. 1818.</strong></p> <p>Standage, Tom. <em>The Turk: The Life and Times of the Famous Eighteenth-Century Chess-Playing Machine</em>. 2002.</p> <p>Gray, Mary L., and Siddharth Suri. <em>Ghost Work: How to Stop Silicon Valley from Building a New Global Underclass</em>. 2019.</p>
