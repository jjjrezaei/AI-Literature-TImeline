# Kenjaku

One of the primary antagonists in the anime Jujutsu Kaisen, Kenjaku, is an important addition to our timeline through how his character prompts discussion of intelligence and bodies as vessels. In this show, there are sorcerers who use cursed energy and different abilities (called techniques) that allow them to perform various actions. Usually, it is different from person to person. Kenjaku is a sorcerer who is over 1,000 years old, which is relatively old compared to most other characters. His technique is the ability to possess bodies through his physical brain. When another sorcerer is dead, he can cut their skulls open and put his brain inside their skull, which allows him to access the dead sorcerer’s techniques, cursed energy, and memories. His character is marked by stitches made across the host’s forehead.  

Kenjaku is an important part of our discussion of artificial intelligence because he prompts questions of how we define human intelligence, how extensions influence our mind, and how bodies operate as vessels. 

Due to Kenjaku’s years of existence and how he moves from host to host, it raises the question of whether he is still considered human. Although he started naturally, when his original body died, did he become something else? What defines someone as human? Is it the consciousness, or is the body? And if a consciousness can transfer from body to body and have the subjective sensational experience as outlined by Damasio in Descartes' Error, does that make them human? If we could transfer artificial intelligence to a human body as Kenjaku transfers his to others, would this qualify artificial intelligence as human through the sensory experience? This character also becomes a parallel for artificial intelligence in the way that he is not tied to a body, and his berth of knowledge has accumulated over hundreds of years. Artificial intelligence sources its information from various sources, as Kenjaku gains his knowledge from his host bodies and his experience within the world. In another way, Kenjaku also becomes the user of intelligence, as opposed to the source of it. He is often mistaken as the source because he has so much, but he is also gaining it. In Computer Power and Human Reason, [[Weizenbaum]] discusses the idea that machines are extensions of our bodies and the use of them alters the affective perception of self. When Kenjaku possesses a body, he gains all of their knowledge and memories, using them as a machine might be used for information. These host bodies are direct extensions of Kenjaku and the affective dimension is much more evident, manifesting in the memories gained. The way that Kenjaku uses bodies as vessels also ties into our class through [[Turing]]'s paper. He is, quite literally, playing a version of the imitation game through his ability to trick people into thinking he is the host. Although he usually maintains his own personality, sometimes he acts like the host and manipulates other characters. This adds to our discussion of artificial intelligence through the difficulty faced determining an identity/machine versus human when that entity has so much information that it is able to replicate another human. Like artificial intelligence, Kenjaku solely understands these memories that form an identity as secondary experiences. Another part worth mentioning is the lack of gender commitment. Historically, Kenjaku is known to have inhabited primarily men’s bodies, but he also inhabited a woman’s body at a point in time. As Turing briefly, and perhaps politically, brushes by, these ideas of the imitation game are not limited to gender, especially when a machine is involved. Kenjaku’s lack of bias for gender, using bodies solely as vessels, continues to break down the binary models of gender and how those models project themselves into current forms of technology.
