# The Montauk Project: Experiments in Time 

June 1st, 1992 - Preston Nichols' book <i>The Montauk Project: Experiments in Time</i> is published for the first time by Sky Books.

With the publishing of the book <i>The Montauk Project: Experiments In Time</i> in 1992, author Preston B. Nichols set the inspiration for the smash-hit Netflix series <i>Stranger Things</i> into motion. The Montauk Project is a conspiracy about a series of experiments in psychological warfare conducted by the United States government, in conjunction with the military, in the remote Montauk Point location on Long Island, New York in the Cold War era. Military facilities have existed in Montauk, New York since the 18th century, but not until Nichols put his alleged experience at the Montauk Project to paper did the conspiracy take full force. His words prompted others to come forward and retell their experience as well, with the resounding conclusion being that participants underwent classified experiments involving mind control, telepathy, contact with alien life, and the opening up of multi-dimensional portals–all under the authority of the United States government.

This account is reinforced by the the existence of the CIA program MK-ULTRA. Operated by chemist Sidney Gottleib from the 1950's to early '60s, the program was in response to fears that "...communists had discovered a drug or technique that would allow them to control human minds (Gross para. 1)." Experiments were covertly funded by research centers and universities, while "unwitting subjects" weathered high doses of LSD, electroshock, and psychological torture, according to journalist Stephen Kinzer, who followed the program closely for its duration. Although MK-ULTRA was ultimately shut down as Gottleib concluded that mind control was not possible, the repercussions likely lived on. Eerily similar to the effects of Dr. Moreau's experiments in the novel <i>The Island of Dr. Moreau</i> by H.G. Wells, the Beast People were the "unwitting subjects" forced to bear the brunt of scientific innovation and experimentation.

In his book, Nichols explains grappling with "repressed memories" from his time on Montauk Point. This reflects the beast people's experience after their surgeries as their animal instincts fight over their newly hard coded inclinations: "Not to go on all-fours; that is the Law. Are we not Men?” (Wells 114). By comparing Montauk conspiracy narratives to the fragmented consciousness of the Beast People in <i>The Island of Dr. Moreau</i>, we expose a persistent critique of imposed control over the mind–a dynamic which extends to how artificial intelligence operates in our modern world, where convenience threatens to erode individual agency, memory, and normalizes the mindless acceptance of authority.

Looking at the development of modern AI systems through the lens of the uncertainty and fear surrounding the Montauk Project as well as the repression the beast-people experienced, both from their own minds, as they fight against their instincts in order to maintain their programming. and from their captors, we learn that succumbing to an entities' professed authority can be extremely dangerous for ones well-being.The beast people are often described as being physically uncomfortable, despite themselves often being the final perpetuators their own suffering: “'They 'None escape,' said the Beast Folk, glancing furtively at each other" (Wells 115). The "furtive glances" expose a collective anxiety that is evidence for lack of true belief. Repeating the law mimics the kind of mirroring that is a key component of many modern AI systems.

Through repression of participants of the Montauk Project and the Beast Folk, we see control operating internally within people's minds and bodies, as well as acting externally upon them. The difference between participants of the Montauk Project and the animals rendered into man-like beings, is that we are not unwitting. But mere awareness is not enough to prevent complicity.
