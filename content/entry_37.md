# Entry 37

<p>The Terminator</p><p>The Terminator is a techno horror thriller from 1984 directed by James Cameron. The film uses a variety of science fiction conventions to tell a cautionary story based on the fear of technology and humanity’s ultimate fate in the nuclear age.  There are two antagonists of the movie, one is the titular Terminator a robotic assassin from the future that can infiltrate society and cannot be stopped.  The other antagonist is less visible as it is only mentioned in dialogue and flashbacks.  The rogue Artificial Intelligence known as Skynet is responsible for the nuclear apocalypse of the future and is almost successful in its bid to end the human race. The film became a hit and became a popular culture phenomenon spawning many sequels a TV series, video games, and even pinball machines.   James Cameron used old techniques from classic horror combined with new technology, Artificial Intelligence, and the threat of nuclear war to warn humanity in hopes to avoid a future that we seem to be circling.</p><p>The T-800 played by Arnold Schwarzenneger  is an amalgation of different monsters from classic literature mixed with computer based intelligence.  The T-800 is a cyborg that has a metallic cybernetic skeleton surrounded by living human flesh.  The hybrid of human organics and machine bears many similarities to Frankenstien’s monster, as both are tall lumbering hybrid creations that resemble man. The Terminators flesh eventually peels off little by little and begins to die with flies clinging to the not quite dead but not really living being.  Unlike Frankenstein’s monster, the T-800 cannot be reasoned with or bargained with, it feels no emotion and has no agency beyond it’s programming.   In the film a lone resistance fighter from the future sent back to the present does what he can to stop the T-800, although he doubts his ability to permanently end the threat, which is another analogue to Victor’s quest in Frankenstein where he tries in vain to end the creatures life. </p><p> Although the Terminator is a dangerous monster capable of incredible violence it’s biggest asset is its ability to blend in with humans.  The film never shows the terminator undergo a proper turing test, but we do see that it can fool many actual humans in key moments of the plot.  One such moment is in the police station scene popular for the line “I’ll be back”.  In the police station a therapist talks to the protagonist Sarah Connor as she tells him how the robot tried to kill her and that it is a robot not a human.  The police don’t believe her story as they watch security footage of the terminator killing.  The police believe that the monsters aversion to pain is due to PCP, and that the ability to withstand multiple shotgun blasts to the chest is due to body armor. The T-800 fools the police well and manages to keep its disguise in place even as it massacres the entire station.</p><p>A different key moment that more closely resembles the imitation game that Alan Turing envisioned occurs when Sarah Connor calls her Mom over the phone. She calls her mom to let her know she is at a safe motel.  Her mother begs her to tell her where she is staying as she is worried about Sarah. The camera pans over the wrecked home of Sarahs mother where the terminator sits emotionless holding the phone as it replies “I love you too honey” in an eerily convincing perfect tone.  This encapsulates artificial intelligence as it imitates humans who are so easily fooled.</p><p>The T-800 while frightening represents a much smaller portion of the nightmare in the film.  The true final boss of the film is Skynet; a self aware rogue Artificial intelligence that had access to the nuclear codes.  Skynet is never directly shown in the film, but is said to be the reason for starting the war against humanity and for sending the Terminator itself back in time.  By removing humanity from the equation of mutually assured destruction Skynet does the unthinkable and launches Nuclear weapons in an attempt to destroy humanity for a future of machine supremacy.  Combining AI with the sublime terror of nuclear apocalypse makes the audience wonder whether we can fully trust those without empathy or feelings.</p>
