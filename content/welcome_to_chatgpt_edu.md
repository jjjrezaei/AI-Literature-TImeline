# Welcome to ChatGPT Edu!

On March 10th, 2025, SDSU announces GPT-4o AI technology, usually reserved for paid subscribers, as available to students, faculty, and staff at no cost. <p>In Mary Shelley’s novel, <i>Frankenstein</i>, readers are faced with the ramifications of unchecked innovation. In Dr. Frankenstein’s immediate rejection of his newborn creation, we see emotional damage as the monster grapples with deeply melancholic feelings of isolation. Then again, we see physical damage, as in a last and desperate recourse, the monster turns to violence and murder. Readers understand that the miracle of the creature’s existence is rendered obsolete as a result of his mistreatment. If proper time and care had been taken to learn of and from the creature, a much different outcome could have ensued. But as all responsibility is shirked over his creation, the creator’s pursuit of innovation becomes harmful and dangerous–a regression in the advancement of morality, and many of the characteristics that give humans our humanity. “Learn from me, if not by my precepts, at least by my example, how dangerous is the acquirement of knowledge, and how much happier that man is who believes his native town to be the world, than he who aspires to become greater than his nature will allow” (Shelley 80). This excerpt from early on in Dr. Frankenstein’s narration describes the real dangers of the kind of reckless advancement that is driven by ambition rather than by ethical responsibility. Ignoring the moral consequences of his science is the inevitable cause of his demise, and it rings out a warning our current time is currently disregarding.</p>

<p>Like San Diego State’s surrender to the prevalence of AI by granting full access to it’s most well-known platform, ChatGPT-4o, the universities pursuit to fall in line with innovation, rather than critical engagement and instruction with a technology that is largely ambiguous to the general public, has potential to become harmful and dangerous, as students move through a higher learning institution reliant on a technology they do not understand, and one that is powerful beyond their knowledge. San Diego State University has consistently ranked among one of the leading institutions of higher education in the country. When an institution that is often viewed in such high regard begins to not only offer, but champion, unfettered access to new tools, said tools are generally deemed to be inherently trustworthy. This requires that we ask questions of what is offered to us, or risk blind and mindless engagement. Offering unfettered access to AI in a school setting does not heed the warning of Frankenstein, revealing the dangers of embracing technology out of a desire for convenience, without the proper critical guidance required to ensure usage that is not only conscientious, but productive. This decision also reflects a broader concession in our society of submitting to fears and uncertainties, and choosing the easiest path forward, even as the destination looms darkly ahead.</p> <p>Shelley, Mary Wollstonecraft. <i>Frankenstein</i>: or, <i>The Modern Prometheus</i>. 1818 text, edited by J. Paul Hunter, Broadview, 2006.</p>
