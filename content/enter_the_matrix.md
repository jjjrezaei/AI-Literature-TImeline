# Enter The Matrix

In 1999, The Matrix (Directed by the Wachowskis) introduced us to a world where AI imprisoned humanity in a simulated reality while harvesting their bodies’ energy. What seemed like a Hollywood sci-fi action film to entertain is now a reality in which AI has become a distinctive feature of our everyday lives. What makes the Matrix so important is how quintessential and ahead of its time it was, and how these predictive insights and blueprints of the future are playing out in the world we see today. The line between fiction and reality is becoming blurrier every day. The central premise of the film is quite simple, really. Machines rose to dominate, enslaving humans in a simulated reality, known as The Matrix, while the machines harvest the human body for energy supplies. The reality is the world is now a dystopian savage wasteland where a select few are able to spectate themselves from the altered reality and fight back the machines and ultimately save humanity. The concept may seem far-fetched, but when we look at our daily lives through the lens of dependence on artificial intelligence, it doesn’t seem that crazy at all. Just like The Matrix we too have become part of a simulated reality where AI has exploited our daily activities and interest through social media, traveling, what to watch, how to buy, and even date, causing dopamine levels in the brain to release when satisfied by this technological advance and in return AI knows more about our wants and psychology than the human person itself. “From the perspective of AI researchers, several aspects of the film ‘The Matrix’ are noteworthy: the advanced capabilities of the AI systems, ‘Agent Smith’ and the robots, as well as the fact that humans in the Matrix are almost completely monitored. Perhaps with the difference that today, in reality, we voluntarily disclose our data and trust that it will not be misused by corporations, government agencies, and those in power.” (Dr. Ralph Ewerth, AI in Cinema: “The Matrix” and Society’s Debate on Artificial Intelligence) Today’s AI system might not control our physical reality, but like the Matrix, it does have a huge influence on how we see the world. AI determines what news we see, the information we find when we search for something, and in return, the user understands this information as “truth.” If the viewer never sees certain information or news, then it may as well not exist, creating reality tunnels that are simply personalized information, and this is what those in The Matrix, physically and mentally, use to function, allowing this mirage state of mind. Dr. Ralph Ewerth states, “The film also raises philosophical questions: What is reality? Can we trust our senses or news, images, and videos? … And other questions: What is free will, can we decide freely?” In The Matrix, Agent Smith, the robots, and the people in the Matrix are almost completely monitored. Perhaps with the difference that today, in reality, we voluntarily disclose our data and trust that it will not be misused by corporations, government agencies, and those in power. The Matrix is a clear warning of what could happen, but if humans can harvest this complex technology to benefit mankind, then AI would be more powerful than any nuclear weapon. With great power comes great responsibility (not a quote from The Matrix), and it is important that we view AI as a supporting tool for critical decisions—such as in medicine, justice, or education—while the final decision should remain with humans, but if we ignore the power of AI we too may have to decide between the red pill and the blue pill and see just how far this rabbit hole goes.
