# ECL 510B: AI Literatures

<p>In Dr. Jessica Pressman's AI in Literature Class, which at the time of writing this, is currently ongoing, we are not only learning ways to talk about AI chatbots, but also their history, aesthetics, and influences. The thesis for why the class exists can be found in the “Course Description” page on the class’s WordPress site. This website is being used and paid for by Dr. Pressman outside of Canvas, the standard learning management system for the rest of San Diego State University. Dr. Pressman writes on the purpose of the class, how it will be studied, and why that is important, saying,</p>
<p>“Literature is the human record of imagining the future, reflecting on the past, and critiquing the present, and literary studies offers a means of exploring what it means to be human, how intelligence renders us human (or not), and what “artificial intelligence” might mean…From philosophy to science fiction, cybernetic theory to cultural theory, digital literature to film, we learn about AI via canonical works of writing. This course provides a historical and theoretical foundation for grappling with one of the biggest issues of our time by reading, carefully and closely, the longer history of AI Literatures.”</p>
<p>The class title, “AI Literatures” is broken down in this paragraph into its definitions, “human record” and "artificial intelligence”, respectively, and then broken down further into a questioning of the words’ own definitions. The outcome of the course is to provide a foundation for discussing those subjects. 
These discussions are framed by the structure of the class, which follows a weekly pattern of writing blog posts on the reading material the weekend before we discuss it, then commenting on each other's posts, and finally basing the classroom discussion on the subjects we brought up in the blogs. In the “Assignments” page on WordPress, Dr. Pressman details why it is important to complete the blogs,</p>
<p>“This is your class, and your participation is vital to its success. You must attend all class meetings in order to receive full credit for participation and come to class prepared to discuss the text in depth; this means having read the entire text before class. I expect you to speak and listen.
Your participation grade includes engaging in dialogue in class. This means speaking and listening actively, attentively, and respectfully. 
We will be doing important learning activities in class—learning digital tools, annotation techniques, and more– so you will not want to miss class meetings, as this learning cannot be made up.”</p>
<p>The expectations are clearly stated and explained. This is to emphasize the importance of how we use our time, especially our time physically in the classroom. During class time, we follow a pattern of small group discussion that informs large group discussion, both of which inform the focus of what Dr. Pressman will then lecture on. She writes about the blog posts on the "Assignments" page, saying, </p>
<p>“The goal of the blog posts is to extend the conversation and coverage of the course.”</p>
<p>Though Dr. Pressman does have a statement about not using AI to cheat or plagiarize, it is more important to have a statement like these, enacting the thesis of the class. Rather than just policing our AI usage, which all teachers are required to do per the university’s standards, she works in the principles of it clearly in the syllabus. We are not only required to “not cheat”, we are required to converse.</p>
<p>This system promotes thinking critically on students’ own work, making it incredibly hard to use AI chatbots to completely fabricate assignments. Even if one is used to help, say, for grammar checking or, prompting ideas, critical thinking will still have to happen on the student's part to participate in the discussions, or reply to blog posts. This combined with the material focused on in class being about the history of these chatbots, how they have been used, and why they are used, makes students more informed about how to approach them. It is this culture that supports the creation of the timeline project in the first place.</p>
<p>I chose this class above any other class that Dr. Pressman has taught (or I have taken at SDSU) because it is an example of a class that is about what it teaches. Since writing this timeline has forced me to think about our context as our content, it would be remiss if I did not mention Marshall McLuhan’s chapter “The Medium is the Message” from his book, Understanding Media: The Extensions of Man. He details how mediums often remain midden behind their content:</p>
<p>“Our conventional response to all media, namely that it is how they are used that counts, is the numb stance of the technological idiot. For the “content” of a medium is like the juicy piece of meat carried by the burglar to distract the watchdog of the mind. The effect of the medium is made strong and intense just because it is given another medium as “content.” The content of a movie is a novel or a play or an opera. The effect of the movie form is not related to its program content. The “content” of writing or print is speech, but the reader is almost entirely unaware either of print or of speech (8).”</p>
<p>This class is not simply an “anti-AI” or “pro-AI” debate. It gives a contextual history to its participants, offering a chance for them to operate at the same complexity of the subjects they are discussing. The medium is also <i>it’s</i> message. In a class where we are already learning about why it’s important to see the history of AI, how it was made, why it was made, and what it looks like, isn’t it also important to be aware of how we are in the class, why we are taking it, with whom is in it, and what it looks like?</p>
<p>This formula of lecture, discussion, and blogging/solo writing can happen in any order and in different amounts class to class; with Dr. Pressman leading the flow based on our focus each day. Therefore, our class is motivated to complete the assignments so they can participate in discussion, and not just completion for completion's sake. This class deserves to be on our timeline as an example of classroom culture that both explains the principles of AI as comparative literature and is a practice of those values. It gives us ways to engage with AI history and literature that are discussive, informed, and prompt critical thinking.</p>
